## What Is a Lay Bet?

A normal bet at a bookmaker is a "back" bet. You're betting that something will happen, such as Arsenal winning.

A lay bet is the opposite. You're betting that something *won't* happen. You can't usually do this at a high street bookmaker. You do it on a betting exchange, where you act as the bookie and other users back against you.

When you lay, you accept a "liability". That's the amount you'll pay out if the outcome does happen. It's held from your exchange balance until the event settles, so you need enough funds in your account to cover it.

## Matched Betting Calculator Lay Bet Explained: The Inputs

Most calculators ask for roughly the same information. Here's what each box means:

- **Back stake:** what you're betting at the bookmaker.
- **Back odds:** the bookmaker's decimal odds for your selection.
- **Lay odds:** the exchange's odds to lay the same selection.
- **Commission:** the percentage the exchange takes from winning lay bets. This varies between exchanges and accounts, so check your own rate.
- **Bet type:** usually "qualifying bet" or "free bet". Free bets often come as "stake not returned", which changes the maths.

From these, the calculator gives you a **lay stake** (what to enter on the exchange) and a **liability** (what you risk there). It also shows the expected result whichever way the event goes.

## How the Numbers Are Worked Out

For a standard qualifying bet, the lay stake formula is:

**Lay stake = (back stake × back odds) ÷ (lay odds − commission)**

Commission is entered as a decimal, so 2% becomes 0.02.

Take an illustrative example. You back £10 at odds of 3.0 and lay at 3.1, with 2% commission. The lay stake works out at roughly £9.74. Your liability is the lay stake multiplied by (lay odds − 1), so about £20.45.

If your selection wins, you collect £20 profit from the bookmaker and pay £20.45 on the exchange. That's a loss of about 45p.

If it loses, you lose your £10 at the bookmaker but win £9.74 on the exchange, minus commission. That's about £9.55, so again roughly a 45p loss.

That small, balanced loss is called a "qualifying loss". It's the cost of unlocking a bookmaker's offer. For stake-not-returned free bets, the formula uses (back odds − 1) instead of the back odds. That's why choosing the right bet type in the calculator matters.

## Common Lay Bet Mistakes That Cost Money

The calculator is only as good as what you type into it. These errors catch people out:

1. **Backing instead of laying.** On the exchange, back and lay buttons sit side by side, usually in different colours. Clicking the wrong one leaves you with two bets on the same outcome.
2. **Entering liability as the stake.** Some exchanges let you toggle between stake and liability. Mixing them up skews everything.
3. **Odds moving.** Prices change quickly. If you lay at different odds from those you entered, recalculate before placing the second bet.
4. **Unmatched lay bets.** A lay bet that isn't fully matched leaves you partly exposed. Check it shows as matched.
5. **Wrong bet type.** Choosing "qualifying" when it's a stake-not-returned free bet gives you the wrong lay stake.
6. **Misreading offer terms.** Minimum odds, eligible markets and expiry dates differ. Always check the current terms on the bookmaker's site.

Any of these can lose you real money, sometimes far more than the profit you were aiming for.
